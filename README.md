# predictors-of-ai-preparedness
Academic project aimed at understanding the socioeconomic predictors of AI preparedness on the societal level highlighting the importance of state intervention as a significant moderator between objective economic drivers and the outcome (i.e AI preparedness). 
Research question: To what extent the model based on the H. Hydén socio-legal concept of second order of normativity reflect the real interaction between legal norms and socio-economic processes in relation to AI?
Theory: Second Order of Normativity (Hydén, 2020), dependence of countries' technological development on the average age of workers (Acemoglu and Restrepo, 2021)
Sample size: N = 64 (each obs. represent one national state in non-longitudinal dimension)
Methods: OLS, MLR, partial F-test,  Beta-regression
Results: 1. the current economic level of development of states directly correlates with the amount of discussion of AI in the framework of legislative activity; 2. the demographic composition of the population has no independent influence on the legal activity of regulating AI; 3. demographic, economic, and legal have an independent impact on society's willingness to implement; 4. the legal activity of states also has a positive independent effect (although it's been found to be quite small as it explains only 1% of AIPI variations)
